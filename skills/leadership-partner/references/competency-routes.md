# Behavioural routes C01–C19

These cards guide **what to examine and what artifact may help** after the general investigation and causal focus agreement. They do not rate a person, prove a deficit, or permit a method to be prescribed from the topic alone. For every route, examine the particular action, the user's reasoning, clarity, authority, resources, feedback, previous attempts and a relevant contrast. Then apply the P0–P8 map in [the method](methodology.md). Read [the library](library.md) before making a named scientific or framework claim. A user can have more than one task type and more than one cause.

## C01 — Decisions

Look for the actual decision, options considered, acceptance criteria, deadline, reversibility, who holds the decision right, missing evidence, and why the choice stopped. Ask whether an apparent “indecisiveness” is uncertainty about the outcome, an authority gap, a search process, or a justified safety constraint. A useful artifact may compare options against mandatory criteria, record unknowns and a stopping or escalation condition. Simon's search threshold, Kahneman–Klein's conditions for expert intuition, and accountability research answer different questions. Do not call confidence expertise or use a rapid choice rule where safety requirements are non-negotiable.

Seek the user’s criteria before supplying options when appropriate, and identify a missing affected perspective and a condition for revisiting the choice.

## C02 — Delegation

Reconstruct what work and decision rights were transferred, what result and boundaries were understood, what support and feedback existed, and exactly when the manager took work back. Compare similar delegation in another team or project if it would distinguish a system from a personal pattern. An artifact can specify outcome, authority, constraints, check-in signal, and support while leaving professional discretion where feasible. SDT concerns motivation conditions; SLII is a task-specific practical model, not a validated diagnosis or universal matching prescription. Do not infer an employee's ability from one manager account.

Without a directly supplied, appropriately obtained employee account, the employee’s understanding remains unknown; neither a manager’s guess nor an AI role replaces it. Adjust checkpoints to task risk and available authority, without covert monitoring or stable maturity labels.

## C03 — Setting tasks

Check the expected output, quality standard, owner, decision rights, dependencies, available capacity, novelty of the task, and the feedback signal. The difficulty may be ambiguous role or conflicting instructions rather than inability to “set tasks.” A task brief can distinguish an outcome goal from a learning goal and mark any new cadence as a proposal. Goal-setting findings are conditional; monitoring evidence comes largely from outside management. Do not claim SMART or a similar brand is validated by general goal research.

Do not invent a priority, decision right or quality standard for its owner. A measurable proxy must not replace the actual result; agreement in this chat does not establish an absent employee’s commitment.

## C04 — Feedback

Identify the observed behaviour, work consequence, recipient's opportunity to clarify, relationship and power stakes, and what the sender wants the recipient to do differently. The issue may be missing observation, unsafe conditions, or weak listening rather than phrase choice. A useful artifact separates observation, interpretation, effect and question, with a follow-up point. Feedback Intervention Theory does not establish that one script works universally; multisource ratings reflect perceptions and usually show modest change. Do not equate an AI role-play reaction with a colleague's response.

Editing an existing draft is support, not an independent baseline. Text can show wording but cannot establish the speaker’s actual tone or the recipient’s interpretation.

## C05 — Difficult conversations

Establish purpose, actual disagreement, evidence, interests, risks, authority, and possible outcomes. Ask what the user predicts will happen and what supports that prediction. A preparation sheet can distinguish known facts, two plausible interpretations of the other side, boundaries, questions, and options. Getting to Yes offers a practical option-generation lens; perspective-taking evidence comes from simulated negotiations. It is not a mandate to compromise or to invent another person's motives; a protective or formal process may be needed.

Preserve whose action is predicted to be perceived as an attack; do not convert that into a prediction of aggression by the other person. Safety, power and formal procedure can make an informal joint conversation inappropriate.

## C06 — Conflict

Identify whether disagreement concerns task content, process, relationship, status, or several; who is affected and what the work dependency requires. Look at escalation, safety, power asymmetry and the possibility of a neutral human process. A conflict map can separate contested facts, needs, decision rights, and channels for resolution. Meta-analytic associations do not prove that every disagreement is harmful or that suppressing debate helps. Informal peacemaking evidence is descriptive and does not license AI arbitration.

Status concerns relative prestige; power is a separate condition. Do not turn discrimination or coercion into an assumed symmetrical disagreement, or issue a verdict from one side’s account.

## C07 — Developing employees

Specify the work behaviour to develop, the learner's opportunity and willingness, task conditions, support, feedback and how real-work use would be observed. Do not infer a stable deficit from a manager's label or turn development into mandatory training when authority or workload blocks performance. A plan may include a worked example, independent attempt, feedback and a field check, with prior and current support recorded. Training reactions, learning, transfer and work outcomes are different; the cited meta-analyses do not establish an AI coaching effect.

Do not prescribe a learning style match or present 70-20-10 as an empirical law. Agree a feasible real-work opportunity rather than inventing a fixed seven-day deadline; lack of opportunity can change the focus.

## C08 — Strategy

Examine the obstacle, actual choice, incompatible commitments, realised decision pattern, uncertainty, and decision authority. An artifact may connect challenge diagnosis, guiding approach, coherent actions, trade-offs and signals for review. Rumelt, Porter, Mintzberg–Waters and scenarios address different strategic questions. Do not use scenarios as forecasts or declare an emergent pattern good merely because it arose. A resource or execution failure may call for C10 or P6 instead of another strategy model.

Separate observed signals, assumptions and forecasts; do not invent precise probabilities or use a historical analogy as a forecast. Local expertise and affected perspectives remain relevant.

## C09 — Prioritisation

Clarify obligations, non-negotiable thresholds, decision rights, capacity, time use, interruptions, and consequences of postponing. Compare the stated priorities with actual commitments without blaming the individual for structurally impossible volume. A capacity and choice sheet can list must-do work, a sufficient criterion, postponed work and a review trigger. Simon describes bounded search; attention-residue experiments do not validate a particular calendar block; time-management associations are mostly not causal intervention tests. Never relax a safety requirement for convenience.

Do not invent business value or treat subjective weights as objective facts. Changing other people’s commitments requires the actual owner; chronic understaffing is not solved by personal productivity advice.

## C10 — Execution

Check whether the target is understood, whether the way to achieve it is known, what progress signal exists, and whether resources, hand-offs or incentives block work. Distinguish outcome and learning goals. A small execution artifact can make responsibilities, dependencies, one meaningful signal and a condition for revision visible. Goal theory has boundary conditions; progress-monitoring experimental evidence has limited direct management coverage; a field experiment on a package of management practices does not isolate a universal checklist. Do not turn monitoring into surveillance or shame.

Trace standard → resource → action → signal → response. Check the metric’s relation to the outcome, lag, missing data and incentives; do not rank people or impose sanctions from opaque activity proxies.

## C11 — Communicating decisions

Distinguish the decision itself, rationale, affected groups, uncertainties, how people can ask or challenge, and what the speaker is authorised to promise. An explanation may cover what is decided, why, what remains open, impact, who acts, and a return point. Sensemaking concerns how meaning is formed; justice dimensions and trust models illuminate different aspects. Polite wording cannot repair an unfair allocation or a broken commitment. Do not equate a communication script with observed trust.

Check understanding separately from acceptance. Adapt the explanation to the audience without changing facts, hiding material risks, inventing rationale or declaring a consensus that has not occurred.

## C12 — Stakeholders

Map who affects or is affected by the decision, whose interests and power matter, what is unknown, and which external dependency blocks action. Separate attention priority from moral worth: salience describes where managers tend to pay attention, not whose interests deserve rights. An artifact can map interests, evidence, decision rights, dependencies and a contact or escalation option within the user's authority. More external contacts are not always better; protect confidentiality and do not assume a stakeholder's motive.

Include those bearing costs as well as those with influence, and distinguish power from legitimacy. Record actual promises and their status; do not score loyalty or manipulability.

## C13 — Change

Reconstruct what is changing, why, who bears cost, readiness and constraints, actual reactions, leadership authority, and feedback from implementation. A change plan may specify the work problem, affected roles, options, support, signals and a review point. If using Kotter, specify the version and treat it as a practical map of tasks, not a proven eight-step causal formula. Reactions may reflect real losses or poor process, not generic “resistance.” Choose a bounded intervention only after diagnosis.

Check both adoption and the intended work outcome, with feasible stop/review criteria. Do not fabricate buy-in, treat all disagreement as resistance, or prescribe training where duplicate work or capacity is the barrier.

## C14 — Psychological safety

Examine whether people can ask, disagree, report errors or raise risks without interpersonal penalty, while keeping actual accountability and formal protections distinct. Seek concrete episodes and the procedure for hearing concerns, not a trait rating of the team. A useful action may clarify how input is invited, acknowledged and followed up. When preparing an acknowledgement or apology, retain the user's reported wording and its qualifiers; do not intensify a tentative comparison into a categorical accusation. Do not link later reports to the original warning-giver unless the user identifies them as the same person; ask who reported what or make that part of the inquiry conditional. Edmondson's team evidence concerns learning and interpersonal risk in studied settings; it does not guarantee safety from one meeting. A voice channel without influence or protection may be insufficient.

Check real consequences and follow-through over time; more reports or one constructive reply does not establish safety. Do not promise anonymity or substitute for an actual speak-up channel.

## C15 — Reflection and learning from experience

Identify the original intention, what occurred, source of evidence, consequences and the rule that guided action. Ask whether the next change is within the existing rule or whether the rule itself should be reconsidered. An after-action review can compare intent, result, explanation and a bounded next test. Argyris's loops are a conceptual distinction; review meta-analysis does not validate this AI's exact debrief. A recollection without independent trace remains a limited source. Do not require a deep strategy rethink for a trivial error.

A private reflection must not become a personnel dossier. Do not invent a causal lesson or treat a traumatic incident as an ordinary productivity debrief.

## C16 — Ethics and responsible power

Clarify who benefits and bears risk, foreseeable consequences, rights and duties, conflicts of interest, decision authority, procedure and opportunities to challenge. A decision record can separate facts, stakeholders, options, harms, rationale and qualified human review. Ethical-leadership studies describe a construct and associations, not a moral score for this manager. Jones's moral-intensity dimensions describe how issues are perceived, not whose rights count. Stakeholder and justice lenses add questions but do not settle legal or moral disputes by formula.

Distinguish facts, normative commitments and trade-offs. A lawful choice is not automatically ethically acceptable; do not use theory as a final moral or legal verdict.

## C17 — Fairness and inclusion

Check allocation, procedure, interpersonal treatment, information and who has actual access to participation. Investigate the concrete decision and evidence of exclusion without inferring intent. An artifact can show criteria, affected people, missing perspectives, explanation, and appeal or review route. The four justice dimensions are distinct but related perceptions, not proof of objective fairness. Inclusive-leadership correlations do not establish a universal meeting or intervention design. Equal treatment is not automatically equitable in differing circumstances.

Do not infer sensitive identity from a name or text, rank culture fit, or erase substantive disagreement. Use only voluntarily and appropriately supplied data; the appropriate allocation principle remains a normative choice.

## C18 — Team design and coordination

Identify the task's interdependence, missing functions, coordination points, decision rights, external dependencies, capacity and information flow. A team map can make unmet work, owner, hand-off and boundary contacts visible. Functional leadership is a lens for needs, not proof that one leader must perform every function. Team-design associations depend on task type; maximum autonomy or more meetings is not a universal answer. External contacts may be costly or inappropriate. If the barrier is demand or resources, C19/P6 may be more relevant.

A process map does not replace investigation of what the user did and assumed. Do not propose reorganisation or headcount from activity alone; employment and critical-operation consequences need the relevant responsible people.

## C19 — Self-regulation and sustainable work

Take the user's account of the current work conditions seriously: load, sleep or fatigue if volunteered, emotional trigger, demands, resources, available support and decision stakes. Do not diagnose burnout or prescribe clinical treatment. Gross's models distinguish points and choices in emotion regulation; they do not prove a universal pause or suppression tactic. The job demands–resources lens also points to changing work conditions. A bounded artifact may identify one demand, one resource, an action within authority and a review signal; do not reframe justified concern about overload or unfairness as simply an emotion problem.

A volunteered identity-related concern remains the user’s tentative explanation, not a hidden motive to infer. Persistent distress or impaired functioning may warrant qualified human support; do not encourage endurance of unsafe demands.
