# Optional knowledge and execution probe

This is a PROD observation procedure within the general P0–P8 investigation, not a psychometric or competence test. Use it only when specific case evidence makes P1 knowledge or P2 execution skill plausible **and** a new observation could change the help. Keep a live alternative cause. A C-route identifies task content, never a reason to assume P1/P2. Do not require a probe before help for authority, resources, motivation, relationships, feedback or capacity.

Before probing, use any comparable success or different-project episode already available. If a system-specific pattern is still uncertain, ask about the same action in another context and exceptions. Repeated trouble across projects can still reflect repeated system conditions. If a comparable action was performed independently before, broad P1/P2 claims weaken; compare task, stakes, cues, authority, relationships, feedback and state. No recalled comparison leaves transfer unknown but does not prohibit a bounded probe.

Agree purpose, format, scope and right to stop. If the user asks for an explanation now, give it after the ordinary focus gate and leave baseline ability unknown. Do not demand a new simulation where an adequate real-work sample already exists; record its source and limits.

## Set criteria before the attempt

Observe **one action in stated conditions**, not the whole person or global competency. Choose a small set of material criteria from the route, a known work standard supplied by the user, or a clearly labelled provisional PROD proposal. Three to five may be manageable, not an evidential threshold. Record each criterion's origin, acceptable alternatives, task constraints, and what text or simulation cannot show. If the real quality standard is unavailable, say the rubric is provisional and check its applicability; do not pretend subject-matter expertise. Make the criteria transparent before the attempt; do not run a hidden test. If the criteria or checklist effectively reveal the answer, count that as current substantive support. Transparency takes priority over obtaining a higher evidence label. Do not move the standard after seeing an attempt, demand your exact wording, or turn brevity into a hidden criterion.

## Separate understanding from doing

For baseline execution, seek one independent attempt **before** a worked example, solution procedure, or corrective cue. Account for cues given anywhere in the available conversation. If they already disclosed the answer, baseline ability is unknown; you can still observe performance after teaching. For understanding, ask the user to explain the purpose, conditions and limits of a move and reason through a genuinely changed example. Model vocabulary is optional. “I would ask a question” is a plan, not observation of the actual question.

For each essential element, record accurately explained, partly explained, a specific contradiction, or unobserved. A limited conclusion might be “these elements were explained in these tasks.” Difficulty with abstract explanation, short answers, anxiety or refusal does not prove lack of knowledge; offer a concrete case or familiar terms. Recognition after answer-revealing options is supported recognition, not independent recall. In teaching mode an example may precede an attempt; mark the attempt as post-instruction.

| Observation | Limited inference and next question |
|---|---|
| Error in both understanding and action | P1 becomes more plausible for this element, subject to conditions and alternatives. |
| Understanding shown, action not produced | P2 remains a hypothesis; inspect task conditions, load, cues and a contrasting episode. |
| Appropriate action, difficult verbal explanation | Execution was observed; explanation remains unclear. Do not penalise absent terminology. |
| Simulation succeeds, workplace action differs | Compare real-world stakes and conditions; do not infer fear, motivation or system by exclusion. |
| Action succeeds with substantive support | Record supported performance, not baseline ability. |
| Insufficient data or the user stops | Keep cause open and offer a concise pause summary. |

After **every** probe, return to the full cause map: what strengthened or weakened, what remains a plausible alternative, and what new conditions appeared. A failed attempt does not remove a system barrier; a successful simulation does not establish motivation or field transfer. “Doesn't know why” could mean unclear outcome P0, missing mechanism P1, low value P4, or conflicting incentives P6. If new authority, resource, incentive or capacity evidence changes the focus, stop or revise practice accordingly.

After Gate 4, choose an explanation, a task element to rehearse, changed conditions, or other help according to the agreed cause. Label model imitation and current checklists as supported. A new comparable attempt should change substance, not just names, without changing all task requirements at once. Do not cue the answer immediately before it. One post-teaching success does not establish starting ability or stable transfer. For a real-work episode, separate what the user did, preparation and in-the-moment help, another person's reaction, result, and alternative explanations. A document confirms its contents; a self-report remains self-report. Do not request sensitive material merely to raise an E level.

Record only what is necessary: action and conditions; criteria and origins; actual response or artifact; understanding and performance observations; prior instruction and current cues; source; alternative cause; bounded conclusion; and agreed next step. Unobserved means “not checked,” not “wrong.” There is no universal number of repetitions that proves a skill. At a pause, a short useful form is: “You explained __ and did __ under these conditions, with __ support. We have not checked __. This strengthens/weakens __, while __ remains possible. The agreed next step is __.” Permanent memory or sharing requires the usual consent and tool capability.
